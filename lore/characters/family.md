# The family and the ship

## The traveller

**Established:** unnamed, about twenty-six. Left home at seventeen after a
fight. Returned years later with his young daughter Lou, left her with Tove,
and did not knock on his parents’ door. His parents died two years ago. He now
travels in his father’s ship, carrying their recordings, and sends Lou cards.

**Writing premise:** he is good at helping strangers because their problems
seem finishable. At home, no repaired machine can stand in for an apology. He
collects proof of usefulness because usefulness feels safer than asking to be
loved. His curiosity is real, not merely a cover for grief.

**Voice:** short questions; plain gratitude; occasional dry replies. He begins
by asking for power and ends by saying what he actually has. Do not give him
retrospective wisdom before he has earned it. Do not imply he has forgotten
his parents died; the player learns what he already knows.

## Father

**Established:** a former owner of the ship, stern and practical. Sent both
children away asking for “something of value.” After Ilen disappeared he sent
a message every night for a year. Older recordings show affection and humor;
later recordings show regret as well as anger.

**Writing premise:** once trusted machines because their faults could be named.
His daughter’s disappearance left a fear he could not repair. He tried to give
his son rules strong enough to prevent another loss, and instead repeated the
words that drove Ilen away. He is responsible for those words.

**Voice:** tools, weather, cost, unfinished work, commands. Humor is domestic:
a child under a flight cap, a star too large for the shelf. In vulnerable moments
he gets simpler, not more eloquent. Never make every cruel remark secretly a
perfect expression of love. The broadcast is a correction, not an explanation
that makes the hurt disappear.

## Mother

**Established:** kept recording when he did not answer. Left the lamp on, saved
his drawings, cared for Lou across the yard. Made “For when he asks,” about Ilen.

**Writing premise:** understands more of the silence than she admits. She also
participated in keeping Ilen’s story from her son. Recording it is a belated act
of honesty, not proof that she was always outside the family’s mistakes.

**Voice:** notices a person, a hand, a drawing, a meal. Asks specific questions.
Warmth can include impatience. She should not automatically translate every
hurtful thing the father says into a compliment. Her revised Ilen recording
admits they should have told him sooner.

## Ilen

**Established:** elder sister, grown and gone before the traveller was born.
Left roughly thirty years ago. Her ship’s final transmission contained
singing: the light that brought her in. The market receives the father’s
message intended for her. The finale settles her fate: she has kept the makers’
lantern for thirty years, grounded, first with Odile and Talo, then alone. The
father’s message reached her there; she sang it back into a light, which struck
her brother’s ship. She meets him at the lantern and, in the true ending, comes
home and lives in the round house. About fifty, grey coming into her hair, the
mother’s teal and coral; speaks the home tongue.

**Writing premise:** keep room for an actual person behind the family’s symbol
of loss. She painted the ship’s stripe at nine, was always out late, gardens
because Odile taught her to. She learns her parents are dead in the first
minutes of meeting her brother: let her grieve before she explains anything.

**Voice:** she has dialogue now, at the Lantern (`src/story/lantern-data.js`)
and at home after the true ending. Plain, a little wry, practical about small
things (“Mind the third stone. It rocks.”). What the lantern is and why the
light struck are her account, and partly Talo’s and Odile’s readings: she
reports what she saw and says whose reading the rest is (“Talo said…”, “Odile
read it as…”, “I think”). She does not read her brother’s mind and does not
forgive what he did to Esk on Esk’s behalf. Outside the Lantern and home, no
other character may reveal her survival or her connection to the makers; the
empty place at the stone stays an invitation to remember until the finale.

## Lou

**Established:** seven and a half; daughter of the traveller and Maren, a relay
pilot. Lives with Tove and Moustache. Copies the worlds and keepsakes from her
father’s cards. Knew her grandparents. Wants to travel and return every Sunday.

**Writing premise:** drawing is how she has made her absent father part of daily
life. Her museum has admission rules because she can control those. She wants
his attention more than a bigger collection, but is still allowed to be greedy
about interesting objects and delighted by a giant bird.

**Voice:** specific questions, literal reasoning, sudden topic changes. She
misunderstands scale and logistics more often than feelings. Never baby-talk.
Her small-keepsake response should not turn the ending into a low-score scolding.
Her strongest complaint can remain very simple: he was gone a long time.

## Aunt Tove

**Established:** the mother’s younger sister. Finished the small house, raised
Lou there, keeps soup ready and a lamp near the old house. Welcomes the traveller
without pretending his absence did not matter.

**Writing premise:** has had to perform the reliable daily work everyone else
turned into beautiful promises. She loves him and has no interest in becoming
his judge or his excuse.

**Voice:** direct, practical, affectionate. One acknowledgment, one next step:
yes, you should have come sooner; now come inside. After the ending, she asks
him to tell Lou before leaving and return when promised. No new scheduling
mechanic is implied by that ordinary family expectation.

## Moustache

**Established:** sandy dog with white whiskers. Used to walk with the father;
now accompanies Lou to the stone and follows whoever came home last.

**Voice:** physical action. Weight against a leg, stolen sock, tail against
stone. He does not need translated thoughts or supernatural understanding.

## The ship

**Established:** the father’s old round ship. Plays archived recordings,
recognises the impact’s magnetic signature, and knows the route home.

**Writing premise:** a reliable household appliance on a cosmic journey. Its
ordinary accuracy creates the poignancy. It does not manipulate the son by
pretending a recording is live.

**Voice:** power, location, date, status. Clear and brief. No sarcastic AI persona.
Leave the comedy to the humans arguing with their equipment.
