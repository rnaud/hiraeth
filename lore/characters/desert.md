# Qanat and the desert

The opening community has a practical crisis inside a sacred tradition. The
water is blocked, and the tree also needs its spark-stone. People have reasons
to care before they have theories about the visitor.

## Nour

**Established:** Qanat’s eldest, Hessa’s grandmother, keeper of the chest for
sixty years after her own mother. Knows the Givers’ traditions and remembers
stories of the old keepers going underground.

**Writing premise:** grew old expecting to hand an unopened mystery to the next
woman in her family. Its opening is a joy and a loss of the job that organised
her life. She jokes to keep the crowd from treating her as an oracle.

**Voice:** affectionate authority, sharp teasing, honest limits. Can explain a
ritual with precision and admit she does not know who built its objects. She
calls the traveller “child” or “little star” sparingly. She enjoys being right,
but delights more in finally learning something new.

## Hessa

**Established:** Nour’s granddaughter, keeper of the well. Sweeps while waiting
for water. Inherited the work, not her grandmother’s patience with it.

**Writing premise:** loves Nour and resents the way the city admires waiting more
than maintenance. Seeing the well fill validates an unglamorous responsibility.

**Voice:** observable facts, dry complaints, sudden unguarded excitement. Her
jokes involve the broom, the timetable, Grandmother. Do not write her as someone
who hates her community; she has kept its well ready the entire time.

## Ama

**Established:** keeps the camp fires for pilgrims, gives the drinking jar,
knows the Speaker and the musicians. Takes care of travellers before judging them.

**Writing premise:** has spent many pilgrimages working while others perform
the important-looking ceremonial tasks. Her humor comes from knowing exactly
how many bowls a miracle will require.

**Voice:** brisk hospitality, fires, food, logistics. She gives water before
questions. Fewer cosmic metaphors than Nour; more instructions people can follow
while carrying something hot. She calls out neglect, including Oum being left behind.

## The Speaker

**Established:** leads the procession and preserves the verses about the giants,
the water, and the carried fire. Knows the marked skull and route toward the Hearth.

**Writing premise:** inherited responsibility for keeping a community together
through a wait with no reliable ending. Ritual provides comfort; he fears that
admitting uncertainty will be mistaken for abandoning the people.

**Voice:** measured and ceremonial when quoting, straightforward when directing.
He can learn that an old verse described maintenance. Never make him deliberately
withhold a useful answer to sound profound. The revised response to the blocked
rib admits someone needed to go and fix it.

## Marrow

**Established:** salvager and self-advertised liar. Examined the wreck, notices
its magnetism, hides a hoverbike under a tarp, gives it away after failing to fuel it.

**Writing premise:** likes being indispensable more than being rich. Frames
kindness as a commercial trick because generosity without an invoice feels exposed.

**Voice:** sales patter, qualifications, magnificent claims followed by accurate
directions. Lies embellish his reputation, not the controls or price of an actual
quest action. When he reports the scar and compass pull, he is unusually precise.

## Oum

**Established:** older pilgrim left behind in the western dunes. Saw the Singer
turn, watched the tree darken. Gives a knotted cord after being escorted home.

**Writing premise:** tired of having her speed treated as a failure of character.
Noticed the sky because she was not trying to win the walk.

**Voice:** patient, mischievous, practical about her body. Ask for an arm; insist
on a slow pace. Never make her confusion the joke. Her testimony is clear.

## The people along the way

These are short voices, not miniature prophets. Their jobs provide both history
and a reason to speak.

| Person | Anchor | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Rima | Walks dunes where the wind erases every track. | A guide’s clear landmarks, mild annoyance at the weather. |
| Pell | Counts bones; Nima’s desert cousin. | Enjoys numbers, knows how much of a giant cannot be counted from above. |
| Rook | Lost a bike with a habit of wandering. | Embarrassed owner blaming an opinionated machine. |
| Ennor | Guides toward the western salt flats. | Water first, directions second, one unsettling observation about shadows. |
| Dalia | Listens to stones before storms. | Reports the unusual dry-night song, does not interpret it as prophecy. |
| The sketching traveller | Has drawn the sleeping observatory repeatedly. | An artist’s curiosity and retired knees; wants to see it working. |
| Tamra | Weaver. | Notices cloth, vibration, and what people insist cannot be touched. |
| Idris | Potter. | Measures a miraculous chest as a very stubborn container. |
| Lula | Baker for pilgrims. | Warmth, flour, feeding an unexpectedly large crowd. |
| Haro | Guards the steps. | Modest authority; responsible for the stairs, not the meaning of the universe. |
| Mim | Sweeps terraces. | Practical surprise at a miracle that interrupts the sweeping. |

The town’s shared chest conversation stays shared. Distinction in those brief
appearances comes mainly from their job, idle line, and reaction; no new branches
were added to give each resident a separate quest.
