# Plot review and possible next passes

These are recommendations after reading `LORE.md`, the story bible, and the
playable dialogue. They are **not implemented quest changes**. The current pass
changes words and supporting documentation only.

## What I would protect

The strongest idea is already here: a traveller goes looking for something
valuable, then discovers that attention, company, and returning matter more
than a trophy. The ancient machines make that theme physical. A city has to
look up. A lamp has to be kept. A bird has to choose to answer. The final
recording can be powerful because the player has spent the game trying to
extract permission from a voice that cannot hear him.

Keep the immense, unexplained universe around these small needs. We do not
need a final lecture identifying the makers. The worlds become memorable when
the person caring for an impossible object has a recognisable problem: Calix
oils a silent bell; Dov is ashamed of where his lunch came from; Wendel has
spent thirty years missing something that happens every dusk.

The risk is repetition. Almost everyone used to speak as if delivering the
same beautiful epitaph. This pass gives them jobs, irritation, pride,
embarrassment, and different amounts of patience. It also names actions and
landmarks directly. Mystery should live in why the light turns, not in which
person has the jar.

## 1. Make the Ilen revelation compatible with the actual route

**Existing issue:** Home unlocks after six completed worlds. The Signal Market
is eleventh in the listed route, and its broadcast unlocks the mother's Ilen
recording only after the traveller leaves it. A player can finish the main
emotional arc without seeing the story's strongest explanation of the father's
repeated demand. The stone scene already handles this optional state.

**Recommendation:** Keep the freedom to go home, but give the market a strong,
early route hook: a recognisable home-port fragment or the name Ilen in a
signal the ship cannot yet read. Let the player choose to investigate. Avoid
forcing all eleven worlds merely to justify one revelation. If the mystery is
intended to be essential, make the route requirement an explicit design
choice and test the ending both with and without it.

**Possible quest work, later:** a pending-signal entry after a few worlds,
with a destination and a reason to care; a clear reminder after leaving the
market that the mother's recording is ready. No new flags or gates in this pass.

## 2. Let the living daughter change what “coming home” means

**Existing strength:** Lou's drawings turn the postcards into a relationship;
Tove has done the daily work while the traveller was away. The two houses make
the contrast between mourning and responsibility visible.

**Risk:** If Lou is withheld mostly for a final surprise, she can feel like an
extra reward for resolving his grief. Continuing to travel immediately after
the ending can also make his return feel ceremonial.

**Recommendation:** Seed her presence earlier without dumping the family tree:
a small drawing tucked into the ship, or one clearly addressed postcard. Let
the traveller avoid the subject rather than letting the game pretend he does
not know he has a daughter. At home, a small act together would make a stronger
promise than another speech: hang a drawing, mend the swing, share supper.
The revised Tove already asks him to tell Lou before leaving and return when
he says he will. Keep her authority earned and practical.

**Possible quest work, later:** an optional household activity and an honest
farewell interaction before another flight. It should not turn parenting into
a completion meter or punish exploration. Decide separately whether Maren
needs an on-screen explanation; her absence should not become a casual mystery
used to excuse his.

## 3. Keep Esk's loss, but make the game's response fair

**Existing issue:** The gate breaks after one correct turn. The flood cannot
be prevented or retried; the journal labels the quest failed. A player can
reasonably read that label as “you used the mechanic incorrectly.”

**Recommendation:** Before the turn, be candid that the old gate is a risk,
without falsely offering a safe technique. Afterwards, the journal should
name what happened rather than imply a missed winning solution. A category
such as “What happened” or a specific “The terraces flooded” entry may fit
better than a failure stamp. Do not turn the loss into a compulsory forgiveness
scene. Esk can acknowledge her part and still want the traveller to leave.

The rewritten lines keep those simultaneous truths. The father's recording
now allows help if wanted and departure if asked; the stone scene accepts
responsibility without treating “I apologised, then left” as the moral.

**Possible quest work, later:** a return visit where Esk chooses whether there
is a useful small job. It must not restore the whole hill or erase the loss.
No flood behaviour, journal status, or repair quest changes are included here.

## 4. Choose what the Singer mystery promises to resolve

**Existing strength:** The matching scar, Odile and Talo's two strikes,
Ilen's last transmission, and the Crystal's new phrase make a coherent trail.

**Risk:** Repeated witnesses all saying a light turned can accumulate clues
without changing the player's understanding. Too many absences can also start
to look like promises of a single final answer: Ilen, the rider, Odile and
Talo, the Major, the makers.

**Recommendation:** Give each major clue a different job. The scar establishes
connection; the second strike establishes a pattern; Ilen makes it personal;
the Crystal establishes recognition. Keep witnesses uncertain about intent.
For a later chapter, choose one concrete question to answer—what the light
was trying to do when it passed his ship, for example—while leaving its origin vast.
Do not identify every missing person with the same phenomenon just to close
all the threads. The present ending can resolve the traveller's decision to
return while the cosmic investigation remains unfinished.

## 5. Make the parents' tenderness and harm coexist

The old suggestion that the father never meant his demand risks invalidating
the wound the game spends hours exploring. The young parents' unconditional
love should reveal something real; it should not prove the later pain was a
misunderstanding.

This pass lets the father recognise that he repeated the words he had said to
Ilen, and lets the mother acknowledge withholding the truth. Neither recording
can obtain forgiveness from the person hearing it now. Keep that limitation.
The traveller may love them, miss them, and decide to behave differently with
Lou, all at once. That is a stronger ending than discovering his father was
right after all.

## Documentation decisions to make before another quest pass

- Treat the opening's cold tree, empty backpack, rib lever, hoverbike journey,
  and spark-stone as the current desert sequence. Earlier hot-tree/fill-and-go
  descriptions are historical.
- Wendel's eggs already hatch in the firefly quest. Retain that discovery or
  deliberately redesign it; do not accidentally remove it to match the old
  “never hatch” note. The Hush-House's flowering is a separate event.
- Preserve unknown fates as unknown. A character's hopeful account does not
  confirm that Odile and Talo got home or that Ilen died.
- If two NPCs share a name, keep their role in the journal and first greeting.
  Renaming could improve recall later, but would need a coordinated text,
  localisation, save/reference, and export review.

My priority would be the route to Ilen, then one ordinary activity with Lou,
then the presentation of Esk's unavoidable loss. The underlying plot does not
need replacing; those changes would make its existing emotional argument easier
to experience in the order the player actually chooses.
