# Enemy roster v2: the camp tribe and the thief (issue #722, proposal, references to pick)

2026-10-27. The author: "the enemies we fight in the game feel a bit boring", and new enemies are allowed if he picks
their references. The plan for #722 (recommendation D14) names two roles the 23 archetypes of
[enemy-roster.md](enemy-roster.md) leave empty:

1. **A camp tribe that carries weapons**, the backbone of hand-placed camps (Breath of the Wild's Bokoblins): it has a
   life before the fight, picks up and drops weapons, throws rocks when it has none, sits round a fire, keeps a lookout
   with a horn, yells when a friend falls, and may run.
2. **A thief that takes something other than hearts** (the Like Like, the Octorok that sucks up a weapon): the loss
   stays with you after the hit, and you win it back by beating or chasing the thief.

Both follow the roster's rules: silhouette first; no body plan shared within a family; the possession shows its own
way; every foe has a job; the body is the telegraph; wildlife is wildlife. Both are **spirits**, the dark taking a
shape (story bible, "The spring-houses": it takes a shape it has seen nearby). That keeps the makers' gentleness: cut,
they go back to ink and nothing living is hurt, so beating a camp of them is a romp, not a massacre of a people.
They bring the spirits to seven, each on its own plan (blob, empty cloak, shadow quadruped, hanging puppet, ember
bud, and the two below).

The prompts for their sheets are in [enemy-roster-v2-prompts.md](enemy-roster-v2-prompts.md); they are run in the
reference lab and the author picks before anything is built (the enemy-rework skill builds from the picked sheet).

## The mummers (`mummer`, tier 1–2; the chief `mummerking`, tier 3)

**The idea.** The dark has watched people's camps for a long time, and in a few places it plays at being them. It
dresses up in what people throw away (a sack for a body, sticks for limbs, a dented pot for a face) and keeps camp:
it sits round a fire it does not need, passes round a cup with nothing in it, posts a lookout, bangs a pot for a
drum. Mummers, after the masked players who go house to house in a folk play: a little troupe acting out a life
it saw from far off. Funny, a bit sad, and never a people the traveller wipes out: a cut one is ink and junk on the
ground.

- **Silhouette:** short and round (1.1 m, a head shorter than the traveller's shoulder), a big found mask that is
  most of its head, long arms reaching its knees, short bowed legs, and always something in its hand. From 30 m: a
  squat lump with a pot on top and a stick held up.
- **The manifestation:** **dressed-up ink.** A body of patched sack and rag stuffed with ink that seeps at the seams
  and drips from the cuffs; two white eyes in the holes of the mask; under the mask, nothing but wet black. Its
  junk is the only solid thing about it.
- **Body plan (new: squat biped):** two short bowed stick legs bound in rag, knees bending forward, flat three-toed
  ink feet; a round sack body; long thin arms with elbows and a three-fingered ink hand that closes round a haft;
  the mask on a short neck, which turns on its own (it looks before it moves). A rolling, bow-legged waddle; a quick
  scamper when it runs. The rig needs a hand that grips and lets go (a weapon as a separate part, picked up and
  dropped).
- **Role:** the camp's rank and file, the foe that fills a site and works as a group. Weak one by one; the threat is
  the group's habits (the lookout, the archer behind, the rocks, the rush when a friend falls).
- **Weapons (picked up, dropped, swapped):** a club (a table leg, a pipe, a tent peg the size of a bat), a spear
  (a tent pole, a broom with a nail), a short bow (a bent spring and a cord; it shoots sharpened sticks). Each camp
  lays spare ones by the fire. Disarmed, it runs for the nearest one on the ground, or grabs one off a fallen
  friend.
- **Attacks:**
  - *Overhead whack* (club). Telegraph: both arms high with the club behind the mask, leaning back on its heels so
    its belly sticks out, 0.7 s; a hop forward as it swings. Counter: guard, and the guarded whack knocks the club
    out of its hands; evade sideways; the combo kills it.
  - *Spear jab* (spear). Telegraph: it plants its feet, draws the pole back under its arm until the butt pokes out
    behind, and the mask tips down along the shaft, 0.6 s; the jab reaches 2.5 m. Counter: parry it (the spear
    breaks), or step inside the reach.
  - *Volley* (bow, from 10–18 m, from a rock or the lookout post). Telegraph: it hops onto something high, holds the
    bow sideways and leans back with the cord at its mask, 0.9 s. A stick flies straight; no landing mark. Counter:
    guard the stick, shoot the archer (a shot knocks it off its perch), or close in: it drops the bow and grabs a
    club.
  - *Rock* (unarmed, from 6–14 m). Telegraph: it squats, digs both hands into the ground, lifts a rock over its
    mask, 0.8 s; the rock is lobbed and **its landing mark shows** (a thrown thing: the roster's one exception).
    Counter: walk out of the mark, or cut the rock back at it like the wisp's fireball.
- **Group habits:** a **lookout** sits on a post or a rock with a horn (a kettle spout, a funnel). When it sees the
  traveller it stands, points, and blows: the camp leaps up and grabs weapons (the alert beat, 1 s, so a quick
  traveller can reach the lookout first). When a mummer falls, the others turn to its puddle and **bang their pots
  and yell**, then rush, all at once, for 4 s. Two of them at most wind up together (the attack tokens).
- **Idle life (the camp):** they squat round a fire in a ring and rock to a drum one of them bangs; one stirs an empty
  pot; one sleeps on its back with its mask over its eyes; one practises with a spear on a sack; the lookout dozes on
  its post and jerks awake now and then. At dusk they dance in a ring. A sleeping one takes a sneak strike. Sites:
  the camps of #722's encounters (recommendation B8), round a fire with a spare-weapon rack and a chest.
- **Low HP:** **the mask comes off.** A charged cut, a riposte, a headshot or the third blow of a combo knocks the
  mask flying; the mummer clutches at its blank wet face, drops what it holds and scampers to put on anything round:
  the nearest pot, a bucket, a fallen friend's mask. Unmasked it cannot see well (it swings wild, and misses). The
  last mummer of a camp with no mask runs for good and melts into the ground.
- **The traveller's answer:** **knock the mask off.** A headshot with the gun or a charged cut ends its fight
  without killing it, and it costs the camp its nerve. Then: reach the lookout before the horn; guard the club to
  disarm it; shoot the archers off their perches; a bomb into the ring round the fire.
- **Tier 2** (from the City-Shaft on): **masked in metal**, a helmet the gun cannot knock off with one shot (two, or
  the charged cut), and one new trick: it dips its club in the fire and swings it lit (ember: the grass catches).
- **Worlds and skins** (route only; the side worlds are discarded):
  - the Desert, the dune mummers: dented brass coffee pots and gourds for masks, sun-faded indigo and ochre rag,
    tent-pole spears; their camps watch the pilgrims' camps from a dune away;
  - the Glass Dunes, the glass-camp mummers: masks of cracked kiln jars, green-glass shards bound on as armour;
  - the City-Shaft, the scrap mummers (tier 2): cab headlamps and hubcaps for masks, pipe clubs, rust and cab yellow;
  - the Buried Machine, the miner mummers (tier 2): helmets with dead lamps, pick handles, ash grey and oil black;
  - the Signal Market, the stall mummers (tier 2): paper lanterns and lampshades for masks, sign boards as clubs,
    the market's teal and amber.

### The chief: the mummer king (`mummerking`, tier 3)

The troupe's big role, the one who wears the crown in the play. Two or three mummers' worth of junk in one body:
**2.6 m**, a barrel for a chest, a big cauldron or a bell for a mask, a cartwheel worn as a crown, a long brass horn
slung on its back, and the camp's biggest thing for a club (a door beam, a lamp post, a mast spar). One per big camp,
from the City-Shaft on.

- **Silhouette:** a wide pear shape with short thick legs, the crown's spokes and the club over its shoulder: a black
  barrel wearing a wheel, unlike anything else on the roster.
- **Attacks:**
  - *Royal swing* (a wide sweep, 4 m). Telegraph: it hoists the beam over its shoulder with both hands and twists its
    whole barrel body away from you, 1.1 s. Counter: roll through it, or the air cut over it.
  - *Throw a mummer* (the Moblin's joke). Telegraph: it bends, scoops up the nearest small mummer by the sack and
    holds it high over its crown, 0.9 s (the small one kicks); then it hurls it, with a landing mark. Counter: get out
    from under; the thrown one lands dazed and is an easy cut.
  - *Belly-flop* (low HP only). Telegraph: it drops the club, both arms go up, it rises on its toes and tips forward,
    1.0 s; a quake ring. Counter: jump or roll back; it lies face down for 2.5 s, crown rolling off.
- **Horn:** it blows the long horn when it first sees you and when it is down to half: every mummer within 25 m comes.
- **Low HP:** the cauldron mask cracks, and it **rages**: no more horn, faster swings, and it starts the belly-flop.
  If all its mummers have fallen it does not run: it sits down heavily, drops the beam, and waits (cut it down, or
  walk away and it fades into the camp's fire).
- **The traveller's answer:** **break the mask first** (charged cuts or gun shots to the cauldron: three cracks and
  it falls off), then the riposte on the stunned king; or **cut down the troupe** before it can throw them.
- **Worlds and skins:** the City-Shaft (a cab's bonnet for a mask, a traffic-light pole), the Buried Machine (an ore
  bucket, a drill rod), the Signal Market (a great paper lantern mask over a bell, a sign post); the main sheet is the
  Desert's, beside the dune mummers, so the author sees the two as one troupe (the Desert itself gets no king: it is
  the teacher).

## The gullet (`gullet`, tier 2)

**The idea.** The dark drinks light: the singing lights drink what a ship runs on as they pass, and the dark, from
far down, thirsts for the same. The gullet is the dark grown into one long throat, drawn to the living jade the
makers left in things, and the traveller carries the most of it: the tank on his back and the fluid shield on his
arm. It does not want his hearts. It wants his colours.

- **Silhouette:** a tall soft tube (2.2 m) standing on a wide sucker foot, with a flared mouth on top like a pitcher
  plant's or a wind sock's, and a bulge halfway up where its belly is. From 30 m: an upright stocking with a
  trumpet top, swaying.
- **The manifestation:** **ink gone glassy with thirst.** Its skin is smoked glass-ink, dark but see-through, so
  **what it has swallowed shows inside it**: the traveller's shield floating in its belly, or the stolen colour
  glowing there like a lamp. No eyes at all; it finds you by your glow (dim the tank, and it loses you).
- **Body plan (new: tube, peristaltic):** a column of soft rings (a crease between each, so the rig bends it like a
  neck anywhere along its length) on one wide sucker foot, a cuff of six soft flaps round the mouth that open like a
  flower, a long thin tongue coiled inside, and the belly chamber halfway up. It moves by inching: the foot grips, a
  ripple runs up the rings, the top leans on. Slow (1.2 m/s), never quick, except when it flees.
- **Role:** the thief. A slow foe that is dangerous because of what it takes, not because of the harm. It turns a
  fight around: kill it before it reaches you, or chase it afterwards.
- **Attacks:**
  - *Gulp* (melee, its signature). Telegraph: it stretches to its full height, leans over you, the mouth flares wide
    open, and loose leaves, sand and spray **stream into it** for 1.2 s (a draft you can see and hear). If it lands it
    swallows him to the shoulders, chews (half a heart), and spits him out **without his shield**: the shield is
    seen in its belly, and the traveller cannot guard or parry until he gets it back. Counter: evade sideways (it
    lands face down with its mouth in the dirt, open for 1.5 s: the dash cut), or shoot into the open mouth.
  - *Sip* (from 4–7 m). Telegraph: it bows toward you, the rings bunch down to the foot, and the tongue's tip shows
    between the flaps and quivers, 0.8 s. The tongue darts at the tank; if it hits, it **drinks the gun's current
    mode** (ember, stilling, bloom or push: never the plain shot). That mode goes grey until it is back, and the
    colour glows in its belly. Counter: parry the tongue (it snaps back and the gullet reels), or step aside.
  - *Spit it back* (only with a colour inside). Telegraph: the glow climbs from its belly up its throat ring by ring,
    1.0 s, and the mouth puckers. It spits a glob of **what it stole**: fire if it drank the ember, a stun if the
    stilling, a push if the push. Counter: guard (if it has not eaten the shield), roll, or cut the glob back.
- **Getting it back:** every blow to the belly bulge makes it retch; a charged cut, a riposte or a shot into the open
  mouth makes it **cough the thing up** at once (it rolls out across the ground, the shield or a jade bead of the
  colour, and comes back on touch). Killed, it gives back everything it holds. The loss is never permanent: if the
  traveller leaves the world without it, it is back with him when he lands at the next one.
- **Low HP: flight.** At a third of its health, if it holds anything, it **flees**: it drops into a fast inching run
  toward the nearest dark water or deep shadow, leaving drips that glow with what it stole. If it reaches the dark
  it sinks and comes up again at its **den** (each site's dark pool or drain, found by following its glowing
  drip trail): it sleeps there, swollen, and takes a sneak strike. With nothing inside it does not run; it
  fights on and gulps faster.
- **Idle life:** it stands in shade near water with its mouth open, swaying, tasting the air; it leans toward any
  lamp, firefly or glowing pool and drifts after it; at a makers' chest it wraps its foot round the chest and
  drinks the glow from its lens (the chest's jade dims), so **a gullet guards a chest** without meaning to. It
  notices the traveller by his tank: crouching or a dimmed tank lets him past.
- **The traveller's answer:** **from range before it is close** (the gun, the boomerang); **evade the gulp** and dash
  cut the bent-over throat; **parry the tongue**. Once robbed: hit the belly to make it cough, or chase it to its den.
  Never only the charged cut.
- **Worlds and skins** (route only):
  - Lorn, the reed gullet (the main sheet): smoked green-black glass-ink, reed-stem rings, a cuff of water-lily
    flaps, lamp-light caught inside; among the lamps and the dark pools;
  - the Underwater City, the bell gullet: a porcelain-blue tube with a frilled cuff like a sea anemone, bubbles
    streaming into its mouth when it gulps;
  - the City-Shaft, the drain gullet: a rust-banded tube that rises out of a drain grating and sinks back into it;
  - the Signal Market, the pickpocket gullet (tier 3 there: it sips twice before it flees): a stocking of dyed cloth
    stretched over glass, tassels on its cuff, coins and stolen trinkets floating in it among the market's lanterns.

No shape is near another on the roster: the shade is tall and empty, the marionette hangs, the blot has no limbs;
the gullet's nearest shape, the root knot, is a low crawl of tentacles, and the jelly floats. The mummers are the
only foes that hold things, so they need the one new rig part (a hand that grips a separate weapon).

Retired by these: none. The blot stays the teacher; the mummers replace roaming packs only at camp sites.
