## The brief (proposals for the author; nothing is wired into the game)

**What it is.** A station of the makers hung inside the sun's corona: a great ring of shades and louvres around a
hollow, cooled heart, slung on cables from three spires that bear the glyph. It is a **listening post**: the sun
sings, low and slow, and the station was built to hear it. The keepers call it **the Shade** (the station's own
name for itself), and the makers' singing lights are said to have been lit here, a note of the sun's song
carried off in a lamp. The sun's pulse beats in threes, like the glyph's three dots and the scar's signature.

**Who keeps it.** The **shade-keepers**: a small, quiet order who live in the station's shadow side, wrapped in
white and gold cloth, with smoked-glass visors and long-handled shade-fans. They turn the louvres by hand to keep
the station cool, tend gardens that grow only in the stripes of shadow, and take the sun's pulse in a great
listening hall at the core. They have never been outside the corona and think of the dark between stars as the
far, cold edge of the world.

**What a visit is about.** The ship follows the light's signature to the place it is strongest of all: the sun
itself. The station's louvres have stuck since the night the sky rang, the heat is creeping in, and the keepers
can no longer hear the song over the roar. The traveller helps turn the shades back (light and shadow as the
world's verbs: walk only in shade, open a louvre to send a beam, splash fluid on a mirror to bend it), and in
the listening hall hears the sun's note clearly: the same one the singing light sang past his ship.

**How it could link to the story.** The keepers have heard a voice in the song once in a long while: a man
talking to the stars (the father's broadcast, which Ilen's light answered). They kept it on a sun-glass plate,
and it plays back as light, not sound. The Lantern's light is one of theirs: Ilen's lantern was lit here, which
is why the light that passed the ship knew the makers' sign. Keepsake idea: a sliver of sun-glass that keeps one
note of the song (a *song* keepsake). A temple gift idea: the **sun-glass lens** (a beam the tool can carry,
or a shade it can cast).

**Its temple.** The makers' house here is **the Kindling House**: a dark, cool sphere at the very bottom of the
station, facing straight into the sun through one round eye, where the makers lit their lamps. Its keeper is a
great moth of light grown afraid of the dark (soothed with shade, not fire).

**How it looks (the Moebius cues).** Blinding white-gold light and deep indigo shadow cut like stencils; heat
shimmer bending every far line; tiny figures against enormous structures; solar flares and prominences as the
landscape (arches, cliffs, slow rivers of plasma); filtered light through shades, louvres, lattices and woven
screens, laying stripes over everything; the light itself as a material (it pours, pools, is carried, hung,
woven). Palette: white-gold, saffron, apricot and rose-magenta plasma, deep indigo and violet shadow, a cool
teal only inside the shade.

Every prompt keeps the house style of the other reference prompts, shows the small cloaked traveller with the
round jade glass backpack for scale where it fits, and keeps light and shadow in the same frame: the world's idea
is that you live in the shadow of the light.
