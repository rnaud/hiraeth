# Temple best practices (issue #400)

The author: *"do some research for temple best practices in Zelda and other games like it"*. This page has three parts.
First, what good dungeons do, with a link for every claim. Second, how Hiraeth's fourteen houses measure up. Third, what
to change in each house, including the plan for issue #401 (one temple across several floors).

How to read the links: a source marked † was opened and read. The others were confirmed only by search results, because
their sites refused the fetch. Mark Brown's *Boss Keys* claims come from transcripts and write-ups, so check the video
before quoting him word for word. Where a point is our own reading and not a source's, the page says so.

---

## Part 1: the principles

### 1. Locks and keys: the dungeon as a graph

- **A lock is anything that stops you; a key is anything that lets you on.** That covers small keys, items, a room
  cleared, a fact you learnt, or a switch thrown. If you draw only "this needs that", you get the dungeon's real
  shape, its *mission graph*, apart from its rooms
  ([Boris the Brave†](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/02/27/lock-and-key-dungeons),
  [Larochelle, Game Developer†](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/depicting-the-level-design-of-a-legend-of-zelda-dungeon)).
  Dormans' generator builds the mission first and then rooms to fit it
  ([Dormans 2010](https://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/dormans2010adventures.pdf)).
- **Choice makes a key interesting.** In Ocarina of Time only one locked door is ever open to you, so a small key is
  never a decision. Link's Awakening lets you hold three keys at once, and from its second dungeon you choose between
  three doors ([Boss Keys: OoT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6LO8Z1DkDqc) via
  [Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2017/01/26/explore-zelda-dungeon-design-in-mark-browns-youtube-series-boss-keys/);
  [Boss Keys: LA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AezAN2RcO8Y),
  [transcript](https://amara.org/subtitles/sA8tGCZJvrxP/en-gb/1/download/The%20Legend%20of%20Zelda%20Links%20Awakenings%20dungeon%20design%20%20Boss%20Keys.en-gb.txt)).
- **The big key does one job:** it opens the boss's door. From Link's Awakening on, that is all it does (same LA
  transcript).
- **Key kinds worth having:** keys used once or used again, keys spent or kept, and "valve" doors that let you through
  one way only ([Emily Short on Dormans](https://emshort.blog/2017/06/28/some-articles-from-procedural-generation/)).

### 2. The dungeon item, and using it again

- **Aonuma's order of work:** pick the kind of play first, then a theme that suits it, then how an item works on the
  rooms ([Kotaku†](https://kotaku.com/how-a-zelda-dungeon-is-made-1686291960)).
- **The item opens the dungeon's second half.** In Link's Awakening every dungeon but Eagle's Tower is full of things
  you cannot pass without its item (Boss Keys: LA transcript, above). The usual rhythm is: the item halfway through,
  the back half teaches it, the boss tests it ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=164160)).
- **An item used in only one dungeon feels wasted.** Twilight Princess is the warning: the Spinner and the Ball and
  Chain are barely used after their dungeons, and the Dominion Rod is tied to the Temple of Time
  ([Zelda Dungeon†](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/daily-debate-did-the-limited-use-of-items-in-twilight-princess-bother-you/),
  [Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=96606)).
- **A tool you keep using gets easier in your hands.** In A Link Between Worlds, which can be played in any order,
  Aonuma says each dungeon brings fresh ideas around its own item. Using items more makes you fluent, and that is how a
  later dungeon can feel easier ([Siliconera†](https://www.siliconera.com/zelda-producer-tells-link-worlds-non-linear-dungeons-work/)).

### 3. Teach, test, twist

- **Nintendo's four steps:** introduce, develop, twist, conclude. Hayashida took them from four-panel manga
  (*kishōtenketsu*) for Mario 3D Land and credits Miyamoto
  ([Game Developer†](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design),
  [GMTK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dBmIkEvEBtA)).
- **Applied to dungeons** (our reading): the first room shows a mechanic safely, the next asks for it under pressure,
  a later one bends it, for example the same block now on ice, or the item used backwards. The Forest Temple's twisted
  corridor, straightened by an arrow to an eye, is a twist on the bow you just got
  ([Zelda Wiki](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Twisted_Hallway)).

### 4. Verticality and floors

- **Every A Link to the Past dungeon has several floors, and holes drop you to the one below.** The manual tells you
  the drops are safe ([Zelda Wiki: Hole](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Hole),
  [Manhole](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Manhole)). In the Tower of Hera you jump down pits on purpose to reach parts
  you cannot reach any other way ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Tower_of_Hera_(A_Link_to_the_Past))).
- **A floor can be the puzzle.** In Eagle's Tower you break four pillars with an iron ball and bring a whole storey
  down, which opens the rest of the tower ([Zelda Wiki](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Eagle%27s_Tower),
  [Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=11020)).
- **Up and down can be the theme.** The Ancient Cistern's statue rises and sinks between a heavenly upper level and a
  cursed lower one ([Zelda Wiki](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Ancient_Cistern)). Stone Tower turns the whole tower
  upside down halfway through, and you explore it again as if it were new
  ([Zelda Wiki](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Stone_Tower_Temple)).
- **Coming in from above.** In Tears of the Kingdom one dungeon opens to the sky, and diving into it is an event of its
  own ([Nintendo interview†](https://www.nintendo.com/sg/interview/totk/03.html)).
- **Seeing a place before you reach it** (our reading; no source states it as a rule): a balcony over a room you
  cannot enter yet, or a chest on a ledge seen from below, gives the player a goal and a mental map. Floors make this
  easy, because you look down onto what is to come and up at what you have left.

### 5. The map and the compass: reading the layout

- **A hub you keep coming back to builds the mental map.** Brown names the central room as a tool for puzzle-box
  dungeons ([GMTK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pwHqY_4nsJ4),
  [Zelda Dungeon†](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/check-out-this-video-exploring-the-zelda-series-puzzle-box-dungeons/)).
- **Writing on the map:** Phantom Hourglass and Spirit Tracks let you mark the map, for example to note where a hidden
  key is ([Boss Keys transcript](https://amara.org/videos/W7sbRTxcf2D2/es/3101067/)).
- **Without a map, coming back hurts.** Death's Door has no map, and a reviewer found returning to a dungeon after a
  break frustrating ([Nintendo World Report†](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/59014/deaths-door-switch-review)).
  Hollow Knight sells you the map, then fills it in only when you rest at a bench, which turns mapping into play
  ([wiki](https://hollowknight.wiki.fextralife.com/Maps)).
- **The Divine Beasts' map is a control panel:** you move parts of the beast from the map. Brown likes the freedom,
  but says it makes some puzzles too easy and does not always show what to do next
  ([Kotaku†](https://kotaku.com/an-in-depth-look-at-breath-of-the-wilds-divine-beasts-1822234173),
  [Boss Keys: BotW](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GudeeRVNo5U)).

### 6. Backtracking with a changed dungeon

- **The best-loved dungeons change as a whole:** the Water Temple, Great Bay, Stone Tower, the Sandship, Sky Keep and
  the Divine Beasts. Brown calls them "puzzle boxes"
  ([Zelda Dungeon†](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/check-out-this-video-exploring-the-zelda-series-puzzle-box-dungeons/)).
  Working one is "like solving a Rubik's cube from the inside" ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=42070)).
- **The ways to change a whole dungeon:** water levels (the Water Temple). A turbine that sends water down
  colour-coded pipes and sets which one-way routes work (Great Bay:
  [Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=55286),
  [Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2019/07/31/tingles-maps-the-great-bay-temple/)). Flipping the building
  (Stone Tower). Time-shift stones that switch rooms between past and present (Lanayru Mining Facility, Aonuma's
  favourite: [Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2011/12/22/eiji-aonumas-favorite-skyward-sword-dungeon/)).
  Moving the building itself (the Divine Beasts, born from Fujibayashi wanting dungeons that move and can be seen from
  far away: [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/archives/260919-zelda-botw-dev-on-fan-critic-response-timeline-placement-divine-beasts-seeing)).
- **The cost is confusion, and the fix is legibility.** Aonuma wanted to fix the Water Temple for thirteen years. In
  the 3DS version the Iron Boots went on and off "in a flash"
  ([Iwata Asks†](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-4-Development-Staff/3-I-ve-Got-to-Fix-the-Water-Temple-/3-I-ve-Got-to-Fix-the-Water-Temple--235888.html)),
  and coloured lines led to the three rooms that set the water level
  ([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2011/05/14/ocarina-of-time-3ds-improved-water-temple/)).
  The lesson: make the dungeon-wide state visible from everywhere, and make changing it cheap.
- **Coming back through the middle:** Snowpeak Ruins returns you to its central room three times, each time with
  something new (Boss Keys: TP, [transcript](https://amara.org/subtitles/d7gY3BK6O78V/en-gb/1/download/The%20Legend%20of%20Zelda%20Twilight%20Princess%20dungeon%20design%20%20Boss%20Keys.en-gb.sbv)).

### 7. Shortcuts and loops

- **Loops, not branches.** Cyclic generation (Dormans, in *Unexplored*) builds a level from loops, so there are no dead
  ends, and a lock becomes "down path A, the key on path B, back round"
  ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/unexplored-s-secret-cyclic-dungeon-generation-),
  [Boris the Brave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/04/10/dungeon-generation-in-unexplored/)).
- **The shortcut that opens back to the start** is the reward for pushing deep. Dark Souls' Undead Parish lift drops
  you back at Firelink Shrine ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dark-souls-1-fromsoftwares-magnum-opus-of-interconnected-level-design/),
  [Boss Keys: Dark Souls](https://youtube.com/watch?v=QhWdBhc3Wjc)). Death's Door is at its best when its zig-zag levels
  open shortcuts back to where you fell
  ([Nintendo World Report†](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/59014/deaths-door-switch-review),
  [Checkpoint](https://checkpointgaming.net/reviews/2021/07/deaths-door-review-a-passage-to-greatness/)).
- **Rest points:** Hollow Knight's benches heal you, save you and let you change charms
  ([wiki](https://hollowknight.wiki.fextralife.com/Bench)).

### 8. How far a clue sits from its lock

- **Let the player choose to look.** Metroid Prime's scan visor holds the clues, so the player decides when to get one
  and the game rarely needs a cutscene to explain a solution. It was added late, at Nintendo's urging
  ([Source Gaming†](https://sourcegaming.info/2017/06/28/holism-metroid-prime-and-the-scan-visor/),
  [NWR](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/news/14884)).
- **Hide some things on purpose.** Tunic puts some manual pages in plain sight and others in hard places, to set how
  much the player understands, and builds "content for no one"
  ([Game Developer†](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-content-for-no-one-an-interview-with-the-team-behind-tunic)).
  Breaking through a wall you thought was the end means there is more game
  ([Inverse](https://inverse.com/gaming/tunic-andrew-shouldice-interview)).
- **Optional hints, and the hardest steps on optional rewards,** are two of Brown's puzzle-box principles
  ([Zelda Dungeon†](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/check-out-this-video-exploring-the-zelda-series-puzzle-box-dungeons/)).
- **The rule of thumb** (our reading): a clue in the same room is a tutorial. A clue a room away, in sight, is a good
  puzzle. A clue several rooms away and out of sight needs a strong reminder: a colour, a sound, the same shape.

### 9. Two verbs at once

- No source states it as a rule (our reading), but the late Zelda dungeons do it. Stone Tower needs all four of Link's
  forms ([Zelda Wiki](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Stone_Tower_Temple)). The Forest Temple's twist needs the new bow on
  an old kind of switch. A late step should ask for this house's tool **and** an older one at the same moment.

### 10. The boss as the final exam

- **The boss tests the item.** In Minish Cap every boss is invulnerable until the dungeon's item shows its weak point,
  and most Twilight Princess bosses use their dungeon's item ([Zelda Wiki](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Bosses)). In A
  Link to the Past only Arrghus needs its item (Boss Keys: ALttP,
  [transcript](https://amara.org/subtitles/eYhYYN1TyE9U/en-gb/1/download/The%20Legend%20of%20Zelda%20A%20Link%20to%20the%20Pasts%20dungeon%20design%20%20Boss%20Keys.en-gb.srt)),
  and Helmasaur King falls to bombs or the hammer
  ([Source Gaming](https://sourcegaming.info/2021/09/17/big-baddies-breakdown-arrghus-the-legend-of-zelda-a-link-to-the-past/)).
- **The trap:** "use the item, hit it, repeat" gets rote across a whole game
  ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=178302)). The exam should ask for the item in a new way, not the
  same way three times.

### 11. Length, pacing, mini-bosses

- **A mini-boss marks the middle** and often gives the item
  ([Zelda Dungeon†](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/crafting-the-greatest-mini-boss/)). Twilight Princess adds mid-bosses
  for pacing ([Larochelle†](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/depicting-the-level-design-of-a-legend-of-zelda-dungeon)).
- **Short has its place.** Fujibayashi sized a Breath of the Wild shrine at about ten minutes
  ([GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/archives/260919-zelda-botw-dev-on-fan-critic-response-timeline-placement-divine-beasts-seeing)).
  A shrine is one idea; a dungeon is several ideas joined.
- **Difficulty grows across the game** (our reading; Brown traces how the NES game's key counts only settle by its
  fourth dungeon: [Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=27509)). Later dungeons are longer, ask for more
  tools at once, and trust the player with clues further away.

### 12. Why dungeons feel alike, and how the best vary

- **The same layout feels the same.** Brown finds Twilight Princess's dungeons nearly identical in shape (Boss Keys: TP
  transcript, above). The Divine Beasts "all feel the same", sharing most of their art and their
  find-the-terminals structure
  ([Zelda Dungeon](https://zeldadungeon.net/daily-debate-are-breath-of-the-wilds-divine-beasts-really-that-bad)).
  Takizawa says himself that the four beasts shared one design, and that Tears of the Kingdom gave each dungeon its
  region's look ([Nintendo†](https://www.nintendo.com/sg/interview/totk/03.html)).
- **Tears of the Kingdom swapped the look but kept the formula:** five locks in the Fire Temple, five turbines in the
  Wind Temple, four taps in the Water Temple, in any order. Den of Geek calls them "painfully easy" and "surprisingly
  linear", closer to a string of shrines
  ([Den of Geek†](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/tears-of-the-kingdoms-dungeons-disappointment/)). The team wanted a
  seamless step from the world into the dungeons (Fujibayashi:
  [Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=201151); Aonuma on aiming for "density":
  [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2023/12/zelda-producer-responds-to-fans-who-want-a-more-traditional-linear-adventure)).
  The temple most often ranked near the top, the Lightning Temple, is the one built from rooms of light-and-mirror
  puzzles ([Boss Rush](https://bossrush.net/2023/07/boss-rush-banter-how-would-you-rank-the-dungeons-in-the-legend-of-zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom/)).
- **How the best vary:** each dungeon has one big idea that changes the whole building. A ship that flips between eras
  (the Sandship: [Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=42070)). A desert split into three areas, each built
  round time-shifting ([Iwata Asks](https://www.nintendo.co.uk/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Skyward-Sword/Vol-4-The-Dense-Desert-and-A-New-System/1-The-Transformation-System/1-The-Transformation-System-221281.html)).
  The Cistern's two levels. Stone Tower's flip. Variety comes from shape and state, not only from looks and theme.

### 13. Other games like it

- **Okami** swaps the inventory for brush techniques and is, in structure, a 3D Zelda
  ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/uk/okami-review/), [NWR](https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/16579)).
- **Darksiders** "openly worships Zelda", and its item puzzles are its heart
  ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/darksiders/b/xbox360/archive/2009/12/29/review)). Darksiders II's
  dungeon pacing comes close to classic Zelda ([GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/?p=56719)).
- **Hollow Knight**'s whole world is one interlocked dungeon
  ([Boss Keys: Hollow Knight](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ITtPPE-pXE),
  [GMTK write-up](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)).
- **Ys: The Oath in Felghana**: dense dungeons with save points far apart and no map
  ([GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/48824-ys-the-oath-in-felghana-review)).
  **Ara Fell**'s puzzles are "tantalizing yet basic" ([RPGamer](https://rpgamer.com/review/ara-fell-review/)), a
  reminder that a Zelda look is not enough.
- **The Breath of the Wild GDC talk** on breaking Zelda's conventions
  ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024562/)) is the context for shrines replacing dungeons.

## Part 2: Hiraeth's temples today

**What was read:**
- `docs/design/temple-redesign.md` (the shrine rules, #336)
- `docs/design/gadget-temples.md` (the gadget in each house, #375 and #377)
- `docs/systems/temples.md`
- the audits up to `docs/audits/temple-design-v1.74.md`
- `src/temples/`

The numbers come from one run of `node scripts/temple-design/audit.mjs` on `origin/main` (0ecf9c9c3, 2026-10-16), and
from the floor heights each house's layout builds.

### What every house shares

- **One template.** All fourteen houses have 9 to 15 rooms and **5 to 9 steps**. Every one has:
  - a few puzzle rooms;
  - the gadget's chest about a third of the way in;
  - **exactly one fight room**, an arena that gives a small key;
  - one loop;
  - a great key for the last door;
  - a guardian with **three phases** that asks for the gadget.

  Every skeleton ends `…LK`: a key lock, then the guardian. This is the Divine Beasts' problem (§12) in another form.
- **Two floors, climbed once.** Most houses have a ground floor before the chest and an upper floor after it, 7 to
  12 m higher. The Footprint is one level, its pool and chasm sunk 6 to 10 m. Only three are tall: the Warden's Well (73 m of shaft over six levels), the Aerie
  (34 m) and the Greenhouse (27 m). In all three you climb up and rarely come back down by choice. Nothing is like an
  ALttP drop or an Eagle's Tower (§4).
- **Few changes to the whole house.** Nine houses have no reversible state at all (the audit's "reversible states" is
  0). In the other five (a stone in one of two vent throats, the Belfry's bells, the Whale-House's horns), the state
  changes one room, not the building. Nothing works like the Water Temple or Great Bay (§6).
- **Every room is the same size.** The shrine rules (#336) make every puzzle room 28 to 36 m across and 16 to 24 m high,
  with doors 7 by 9 m. That is good for the camera and for reading a room from its door. But a Hiraeth room is never
  cramped, long or vast, so the houses lose one of Zelda's pacing tools.
- **No map.** Inside a house the player has only the scout's ping, which points at the next step (`logic.next`). There
  is no plan of the floors and no way to read the layout (§5). The ping is a compass that also solves the route.
- **No difficulty curve across the game.** Steps per house in route order:

  | Order | House | Steps |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Rose House | 8 |
  | 2 | Aerie | 6 |
  | 3 | Belfry | 9 |
  | 4 | Hush-House | 6 |
  | 5 | Lamp-House | 6 |
  | 6 | Greenhouse | 9 |
  | 7 | Whale-House | 7 |
  | 8 | Warden's Well | 9 |
  | 9 | Clock-House | 7 |
  | 10 | Engine-House | 6 |
  | 11 | Casting-House | 6 |
  | 12 | Footprint | 5 |
  | 13 | Mooring-House | 8 |
  | 14 | Undertower | 9 |

  The late houses are no longer and no harder than the early ones. The Footprint, twelfth of fourteen, is the shortest
  of all (§11).

**Already good, and worth keeping:**
- every house has a real dungeon item, used after its chest and by its guardian (#375 and #377 did this: §2);
- teach-test-twist scores 5 in every house (§3);
- keys sit away from their locks (decoupling 4 to 5: §8);
- since v1.74 no door is unfair (none left at 1.0 obscurity);
- each house has an idea of its own: wind, bells, light, sound, clocks.

## Part 3: recommendations

### For every house (the rules to add)

1. **One house-wide state per late house** (§6). Something set in one place that changes several rooms on several
   floors: a water or sand level, power on or off, the hour, a moored room docked high or low. Make it readable
   everywhere: a gauge in every room, one colour per state. Make it cheap to change: a lever near every hub, never a
   long trip back (the Water Temple 3DS fixes).
2. **A curve across the game** (§11). Houses 1 to 4 keep 6 to 8 steps. Houses 5 to 10 grow to 9 to 11, houses 11 to 14
   to 12 to 15. Each later house asks for one older tool besides its own (§9).
3. **Break the template** (§12). Not every house needs one arena, one small key, one loop and three phases. Give one
   house no fight at all, one a mini-boss at its middle that guards the gadget (§11), one two small keys with a choice
   of door (§1), and one a guardian with two phases and a puzzle phase.
4. **Let room sizes vary** within the shrine rules (§11). Keep 28 m as the floor for a puzzle room. Allow one great
   hall per house (60 m or more, several floors seen at once) and narrow passages between, so the house breathes.
5. **A plan of the house** (§5). A map found in each house (in a little chest, like the keys) and shown in the pause
   menu: floors as tabs, rooms seen and not seen, the great key's door marked. The scout's ping stays as the hint for
   whoever wants it, but the map lets the player read the house. The debug page planned in issue #396 can share its
   drawing.
6. **Drops as shortcuts, rest by the hub** (§4, §7). Every house with an upper floor gets at least one safe drop from
   it back to the hub, and the guardian's checkpoint sits one short walk from a mark.
7. **Ask the author about one rule:** "nothing threaded through doors" (#336). A ball **dropped through a hole in the

## Part 4: verticality, the plan for issue #401 (built in v1.82: see "As built" at its end)

### Which house

**The Footprint** (the Garden of Spheres, twelfth on the route). Why:

- **It has the most to gain.** It is the only house on one level (every room at floor height 0 but the pool and the chasm, sunk 6 and 10 m), the
  shortest (5 steps), and it has no backtracking and no house state. It comes late, where the game should be at its
  most demanding.
- **The lore asks for it.** "Something that walked through the sky putting spheres down" left its print under every
  sphere (LORE). The temple is a footprint pressed into the hill. Going **down** into the print is the house's idea,
  and depth is a kind of verticality no other house has (the rest climb).
- **Its gadget is about seeing.** The seeing lens shows what is hidden. On several floors, "seeing a place before
  reaching it" (§4) becomes the lens's job: from a balcony you see a floor below whose stones only the lens shows,
  and you know where you will go.
- **It is small,** so a rebuild costs less than reworking a big house. Its guardian (the Echo) and its puzzles (the
  spheres on the walker's print, the Still Pool, the Hall of the Unseen) carry over.

**The alternative is the Whale-House with a tide** (the Water Temple done right: §6). It is a stronger set piece, but
water that rises through rooms needs swimming in temples, water volumes that move, and Unity parity for all of it. It
fits better as a second house later.

### The sketch: four floors round one well

The print is three toes and a heel. The **Heel Well** is a round shaft 40 m across down the middle of the house, open
from the top floor to the bottom. Every floor has a balcony on it, so from any floor you look down at the floors to
come and up at the floors you have left. The toes are wings off the well on each floor.

```
  F0  0 m   Threshold ─ RIM (balcony round the well) ─ West toe: Hall of Spheres
                │                                    └ East toe: the Keepers' Lodge (arena)
          (the well: drops, a spiral of lens stones, the sphere lift)
  F1 −18 m  Still Pool (west) ─ BALCONY ─ Sounding Toe (east)
                │
  F2 −36 m  Lens Chamber (the chest) ─ BALCONY ─ Hall of the Unseen (east)
                │
  F3 −54 m  the heel's floor: the Echo's door, seen from the rim on the way in
```

**The path, with its choices:**

1. **F0, the rim.** You come in on the rim and see straight down to the Echo's door at the bottom, shut, with its
   great lock (seeing the goal first, §4). West, the **Hall of Spheres** (today's puzzle: the sphere on the walker's
   print). Solved, it opens a stair down to F1 west. East, the **Lodge** is barred by a door that wants a small key.
2. **F1, two ways.** West, the **Still Pool** (today's puzzle). East, the **Sounding Toe**, a new puzzle room: a sphere
   on a groove whose plate is on F2, straight below a hole in the floor. Rolled into the hole, it drops onto the plate
   below and opens F2's east door (the ALttP drop, §4; see "Ask the author" above). Either order works, and each opens
   a way to F2.
3. **F2, the chest.** The **Lens Chamber** holds the seeing lens. Raise it over the well, and a spiral of stones only
   the lens shows appears round the well's wall, from F2 up to F0's rim. This is the **shortcut up**: from now on, the
   whole house is one climb away (§7).
4. **Up to fetch, down to use** (the non-linear heart). The small key is in the **Lodge** on F0, behind its barred door.
   That door opens from inside the well, by an eye only the lens shows, so you climb the lens stones to fight there.
   The great key is in the **Hall of the Unseen** on F2, behind the small-key lock. A key found above opens a door below.
5. **Drops as the way back down** (§4, §7). From the rim, three marked drops land on the F1 and F2 balconies. A shallow
   pool on each balcony catches the fall: no damage, and a sound that says this is the way.
6. **F3, the Echo.** You go down the last flight from F2 with the great key. The Echo's fight uses the well: its spheres
   sit on the balconies above, and from its second phase the lens shows which one wears the print (as today, but
   seen up the well).

**House state (§6):** the sphere lift. One great sphere sits in a cradle in the well and rides up and down between F0
and F2 on its own rail. Rolled onto the rim's plate, it lifts the F1 bridge across the well. Sent down to F2's plate, it
opens the Unseen hall's far side and lowers the bridge. It is one visible object, and from any balcony you can see
where it is: one state you set, readable everywhere.

**Size against the shrine rules:** floors 18 m apart keep every room's 16 m ceiling. The well is a shaft, not a room (as
`rotunda` with no oculus is today), every puzzle sits in the middle 60 % of its room, and the drops land in the middle
of their balconies.

**Expected steps: 11 to 12,** up from 5. It would be the house that teaches the game's last three houses to think in
floors.

**What #401 will need to check:** every drop's landing walked with a real `Player`. The lens stones read from the rim
in headless Chrome. The sphere's drop through the floor accepted or refused by the author. The same in Unity.
