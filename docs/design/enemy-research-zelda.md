# What makes Zelda-like enemies fun to fight

Research for rnaud/hiraeth#722 ("our enemies feel a bit boring"). Written 2026-10-27.

**About the sources.** Zelda Wiki (zeldawiki.wiki), Zelda Dungeon and the Fandom wiki all blocked
direct page fetches during this pass. So the movesets below come from web-search excerpts of those
wikis and guides, plus well-known facts about the games. Where a claim comes from a found source,
the URL sits next to it. Lines with no URL are common knowledge of the games and can be checked on
the wiki enemy lists:

- OoT enemies: https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Enemies_in_Ocarina_of_Time
- BotW enemies: https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Enemies_in_Breath_of_the_Wild and
  https://zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Breath_of_the_Wild_Enemies
- TotK enemies: https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Zelda_Wiki:Tears_of_the_Kingdom/Enemies

All text is paraphrased. Hiraeth's player kit, for reference: a sword combo, a charged cut, an air cut,
guard and parry with a riposte, a dash cut, a roll, a gun with modes, and lock-on.

## 1. Ocarina of Time (1998)

OoT built its fights around Z-targeting. The idea came from a sword-fight show where foes attack one
at a time: locking on marks one enemy, and the others wait their turn. The team made a fight against
two Stalfos to prove it
([TCRF prerelease notes](https://new.tcrf.net/Prerelease:The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time/Early_Sword_on_A)).
So most OoT enemies are duels: a clear tell, one right answer, then a window to punish.

| **Stalfos** | Sword and shield. It blocks most frontal hits with its shield, hops back out of reach, strikes overhead and sideways, and jump-slashes. Often comes in pairs (the first Z-targeting test). | Raises its sword high before the overhead; crouches before the jump. | Patience. Block or sidestep, then hit the opening after its swing. Then counter. | Agile, so you must track them. Being blocked makes it lose its footing: the shield is an attack, not a wall. The pair who "tag in" feels like a team. |

Fear comes from taking control away for a moment and from the sound. |
| **Dodongo / Baby Dodongo** | Big lizard walks slowly and breathes fire in a line. Baby Dodongos rush and explode when killed. | Inhales before the breath. | Hit its tail; bombs; stay off its line. Babies: kill them away from you. | Dodongo's Cavern is built round bombs, and these foes are too. Its death blows up: the corpse is a hazard. |
| **Tektite (red / blue)** | Jumps in big hops. The blue one jumps on water. | Crouches before each hop. | Hit it as it lands; time your swing. | Rhythm. It teaches you to read a jump. |
| **Leever** | Burrows in sand, pops up in a ring round you and spins toward you. | Sand mounds move; dust. | Keep moving; kill it when it surfaces. | The ground is the threat. It pushes you to keep walking across desert. |
| **Beamos** | A tower with a rotating eye that fires a laser when it sees you. | The eye turns and stops on you. | A bomb destroys it. Or move round its view. | An obstacle that is also a puzzle: one tool, one answer. |
| **Armos** | Statue that comes to life when touched and hops after you. | Looks like one statue among many. | Run, then hit it from behind or with bombs. | The surprise: is that statue alive? It turns the set dressing into a threat. |
| **Floormaster / Wallmaster** | Wallmaster: a hand drops from the ceiling and sends you back to the dungeon entrance. Floormaster: a hand that splits into three small hands when hit. | A growing shadow on the floor, and a sound. | Keep moving when you hear it; roll away. Kill the small hands quickly before they regroup. | The penalty is losing progress, not hearts. The split teaches priority. Very memorable fear. |
| **Freezard** | Ice statue that breathes freezing mist. | Puffs its chest. | Fire (Din's Fire, fire arrows), or attack from the side. | Matches the Ice Cavern. Element against element. |
| **Shell Blade** | Clam on the bottom of Water Temple pools; snaps shut, opens to bite. | Shell opens. | Hookshot or iron-boots sword hit on the soft inside. | The dungeon's item and its enemy belong together. |
| **Stinger / Spike / Bari / Biri** | Water and jelly foes. Bari/Biri shock you if you hit them with a sword. Bari splits into small Biri. | Glow before the shock. | Use the boomerang or a ranged tool; avoid the sword. | The punishment for using the wrong tool. It makes you switch. |
| **Dead Hand** (mini-boss, Bottom of the Well and Shadow Temple) | Many long arms grow from the floor and grab Link. When it has him, the head comes to bite. | Hands sway and reach out. | Let a hand grab you on purpose, mash free, then hit the head when it comes close. | It asks you to take the bait. Very creepy. The only way to win is a scary risk. |

**What OoT gets right.** Each enemy has one clear tell and one clear answer. Many answers are the
dungeon's item (bombs for Beamos and Dodongos, hookshot for Shell Blades, fire for Freezards,
Sun's Song for ReDeads). The memorable ones break the "lose hearts" rule. They take your shield
(Like Like), send you back (Wallmaster), freeze you (ReDead), or hide a person (Iron Knuckle).

## 2. Breath of the Wild (2017) and Tears of the Kingdom (2023)

BotW moved from duels to "monster societies" living in an open world. The chemistry engine
(fire, electricity, wind, metal) runs separately from the physics and is not scripted for single
cases. Enemies obey it as much as Link does
([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/the-legend-of-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-makes-chemistry-just-as-important-as-physics/);
[GDC 2017 talk](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024562/Change-and-Constant-Breaking-Conventions)).
Producer Aonuma said Bokoblins look endearing if you watch them closely
([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/?p=90674)).

### Combat verbs the enemies are built around
- **Perfect guard** (shield just before the hit): staggers melee foes and sends Guardian beams back
  ([Zelda Wiki: Perfect Guard](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Perfect_Guard)).
- **Flurry Rush** (dodge at the last moment): slows time for a free combo.
- **Headshot** stun, **sneak strike** on sleeping foes, **weapon throw**, elemental arrows, bombs,
  magnesis and metal, rolling boulders, fire on grass and updrafts.

### Camp and encounter design in BotW
- **Camps are stages.** A skull rock, a watchtower, a fire, a chest, explosive barrels, boulders
  on the slope above. Each prop is a tool for one side or the other.
- **Before the fight:** sleeping, cooking, dancing, a lookout. You can watch and plan.
- **Start of the fight:** the lookout spots you; a "?" moment where it looks round; then the horn.
  You have a few seconds to hide or take it out.
- **During:** they pick up dropped weapons, light their clubs in the fire, throw rocks, ride horses,
  kick bombs back. The group yells to each other.
- **End:** the last one may flee, beg, or cower. A chest unlocks when the camp is cleared.
- **Escalation:** the same species in five colours. Red is the tutorial, silver is the late test.
  New moves come with the colour (blue kicks bombs). The world levels foes up as you play.
- **Blood moon:** it brings back all the camps and resets the world.

Critique to keep in mind: some players feel the camps get same-y after dozens of them
([csanyk blog](https://csanyk.com/2021/02/life-as-a-bokoblin-botw-mock-documentary-suggests-deeper-gameplay-possibilities/)).
Variety came from the stage and the tools, more than from new species.

## 4. Design principles that make ordinary enemies fun

**1. A life before the fight.** Foes who sleep, eat, guard, patrol, talk or dance give the player a
choice of how to start. They also make the world feel lived in. *BotW* Bokoblins sleep with snot
bubbles and cook round fires; Hinox snores with weapons round its neck; *OoT* Iron Knuckles stand as
statues. A sleeping enemy invites a sneak attack.

**2. A clear tell, then a clear window.** Every attack has a wind-up you can read (pose, sound,
glow), and every attack leaves a gap after it. *OoT* Iron Knuckle lifts its axe for a long time and
then stays open; Guardian lasers show a red dot and a ping; Lynels rear up and roar. Fair means
"I could have seen it coming".

**3. One answer that feels clever.** The best simple foes ask for one specific move or tool:
Deku Scrub (reflect the nut), Beamos (bomb), Shell Blade (hookshot), ReDead (Sun's Song),
Molduga (bomb lure), elemental Lizalfos (the opposite element). Tie answers to the player's kit.

**4. Defence becomes offence.** Blocking or parrying should open the enemy, not just stop damage.
*OoT* Lizalfos stumble when blocked; *Wind Waker* Darknuts lose armour to a parry; *BotW* perfect
guard reflects Guardian beams and staggers Lynels; *Sekiro* posture breaks.

**5. States that change mid-fight.** Armour that falls off, a weapon lost, a limb cut, a rage phase.
*OoT* Iron Knuckle gets faster without armour; *WW* Darknut switches to fists when disarmed; *BotW*
Stalkoblins lose their heads; *MH* monsters limp and get enraged. A fight with two halves feels
like a story.

**6. Groups with roles, and turn-taking.** A lookout, a brute, an archer, a swarm. The group
coordinates: the horn wakes the camp, Moblins throw Bokoblins, *OoT* Lizalfos tag in, *Doom* limits
attackers with tokens. One at a time keeps the fight fair; the roles keep it interesting.

**7. Reactions to the player and to each other.** Kick the bomb back, dodge an arrow, flee when
alone, beg, taunt, pick up the leader's weapon, yell when a friend dies. *BotW* blue Bokoblins kick
remote bombs; *Arkham* foes talk about the knocked-out guard. A reaction shows the foe noticed you.

**8. Foes use the world and its items.** They pick up weapons, throw rocks and barrels, light clubs
in fire, ride horses, shake loose boulders. *BotW* chemistry applies to them too: metal draws
lightning, grass burns under them. The player can use the same things against them.

**9. Surprise and ambush.** Statues that wake (Armos, Iron Knuckle), ceiling drops (Skulltula,
Wallmaster, Horriblin), disguises (Yiga, Treasure Octorok, forest Octorok), sand and water lurkers
(Leever, Molduga, Lizalfos camouflage). Use sparingly, and give a hint (a shadow, a sound).

**10. Penalties other than damage.** Lose your shield (Like Like), go back to the dungeon start
(Wallmaster), freeze (ReDead), have your weapon stolen (Octorok sucks it). These hurt and are
remembered far more than one more heart lost.

**11. Escalation through variants.** The same body, a new colour, one new trick. Red to silver
Bokoblin; fire, ice and shock Wizzrobe; Mighty Darknut with a cape. Players already know the base,
so the new trick stands out. Cheap to make.

**12. Weak points you can see.** The Hinox eye, the Talus ore, the Guardian eye, the Skulltula belly,
the Darknut strap. Visible targets reward aim and position.

**13. The feel of hits.** Hit-stop, knockback, a stagger pose, a fall, ragdoll off a ledge, a big
death (Dodongo explodes, Bokoblins burst into purple smoke with loot). The reaction of the foe is
the sword's feedback.

**14. Fear and awe as tools.** A Guardian's music, a Lynel on the hill, a ReDead's scream. A few foes
should be too strong at first and beatable later. The change is the player's growth.

**15. Pacing and rest.** Space fights out; mix easy and hard; let some foes be avoided
([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-better-enemies)). BotW lets you skip
almost every camp.

**16. Rewards tied to the foe.** The foe drops its weapon (Wizzrobe rod, Moblin spear), a body part
(Lizalfos tail, Lynel hoof), or opens a chest. Rare variants drop rare loot (rare Talus). Loot that
comes from the enemy's body makes its look matter.

**17. Personality and humour.** Bokoblins slap their bellies, Deku Scrubs beg and sell, Stal skulls
roll after their bodies, Yiga drop a banana. Humour makes the foe a character and the fight lighter.

## 5. Most transferable ideas for a small team (Hiraeth)

Ordered by value for effort. Each uses Hiraeth's existing kit.

1. **Parry strips armour (Darknut).** An armoured foe takes little damage until a parry or a charged
   cut knocks off a plate; then it changes: faster, angrier, open to the riposte. One body, two
   fights.
2. **Every attack: one tell, one window.** Audit each foe: a pose plus a sound before each attack,
   and a clear recovery after it. The riposte and dash cut are built to punish that window.
3. **Idle life and a lookout.** Foes sit, sleep, eat, patrol before the fight. One spots you, shouts
   or blows a horn, and the rest wake. Sneaking up for a first free hit (charged cut on a sleeping
   foe) becomes a choice.
4. **Roles and turn-taking.** A brute, a fast duellist, a ranged shooter, a swarm. A simple token:
   one or two attack at once, the rest circle and taunt. Mix roles per encounter.
5. **Reactions.** Flee when alone, cower, taunt, yell when a friend falls, dodge the gun when it is
   aimed at them, kick a thrown bomb back (high tier only). Mostly barks and short animations.
6. **Foes pick up weapons and throw things.** Drop a weapon on hit; let the foe (or the player) pick
   it up. Unarmed foes throw rocks. Very readable, very "alive".
7. **Each gun mode is somebody's answer.** One foe reflects normal shots but dies to a charged shot;
   one is stunned by a headshot (Lynel/Hinox) and opens for a mounted or air-cut finisher; one needs
   the gun to break a ranged shield. The gun gets meaning.
8. **Weak point you can see.** One glowing eye, a strap, a back plate. Lock-on can jump between body
   and weak point.
9. **Colour tiers.** Reuse each body in two or three tiers, each with one added move. Cheap
   escalation across worlds.
10. **One non-damage penalty per world.** A thing that steals a coin or your gun mode for a while,
    a grab you must mash out of, a hand that sends you back to the room's door. Use once or twice.
11. **Ambush with a hint.** A statue that wakes, a ceiling drop with a shadow, a foe in tall grass.
12. **Hit feel and deaths.** Hit-stop, a short stagger, knockback that can send foes off ledges or
    into water, and a distinct death (burst of ink, drop of loot). For the Moebius style, an ink
    splash or the foe's line art scattering.
13. **Use the stage.** Put a barrel, a boulder on a slope, a fire pit or a ledge in each camp.
    Charged cut or the gun can set it off; foes can use it too.
