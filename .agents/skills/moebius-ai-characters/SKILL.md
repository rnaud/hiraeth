# Character creation and quality for Moebius / Hiraeth

Turn reference art into a reviewable animated character, preserving identity and proportions while measuring rig and cloth quality. This workflow grew from the coral-shirt Tripo experiment. Its repairs and fit constants are character-specific starting points, not a universal auto-rigger.

Use the Moebius/Hiraeth checkout containing this skill; resolve implementation paths from the repository root. Read its current instructions before changing files. The repo reserves port 5173 for the user's server; the experiment uses 5174. Keep new prototype assets separate from the live traveller until the user requests integration. The accepted coral-shirt v1 is already the default playable traveller.

For procedural people, enemies, or a roster-wide quality pass, read [procedural-quality.md](references/procedural-quality.md). It covers reference coverage, attachment geometry, motion-state setup and the reusable screenshot tools.

Read [pipeline-and-failures.md](references/pipeline-and-failures.md) when generating, fitting or repairing a character. Read [repo-map.md](references/repo-map.md) for the existing tools and validation commands. Do not duplicate those implementations into the skill.

## Decide the asset contract before generating

- Choose one canonical face, proportions, outfit and accessory set from the supplied references. Variant sheets can disagree. Resolve meaningful conflicts; don't silently mix different faces or backpacks. A prior character's no-backpack choice is not a permanent rule for new characters.
- Use the user's chosen service. For hosted services, inspect current capabilities, input requirements, output formats and costs before spending credits; generation interfaces change. Existing credits do not authorize unlimited retries or a new purchase. Preserve project/job identifiers and untouched exports.
- A turnaround generated by an image model is an interpretation, not guaranteed consistent geometry. Match silhouette, face landmarks, scale, camera elevation, pose and lighting across views before using multi-view inputs. Prefer few consistent views over many contradictory ones.
- **Before spending credits on a humanoid game character, require a visually verified T-pose reference:** arms extended horizontally, straight wrists, open empty hands with separated readable fingers, legs and feet separated, and no limb touching clothing or another limb. Preserve the selected character's identity and outfit. Pocketed hands, held props, folded arms and a merely relaxed standing pose fail this requirement. Prepare the T-pose in Midjourney using the approved reference, following the repository's reference-selection rules. Do not substitute a posed generation as the game asset; a posed mesh is only a visual prototype unless the user explicitly requests a static asset or another rest pose. Bake and verify transforms before fitting.
- **Require lighting-neutral base color:** use evenly lit references without directional shadows, highlights or painted ambient occlusion; enable Tripo's **Remove Lighting** (or the chosen service's equivalent) before generation and record the setting. The toggle is not proof of clean textures. Inspect the exported base-color map on an unlit mesh, then relight from opposite directions with ambient occlusion disabled. Correct or regenerate any persistent lighting before game acceptance; changing roughness, disabling PBR or turning off runtime shadows cannot remove baked lighting. Preserve intentional ink contours without treating broad painted shadows as linework. See [pipeline-and-failures.md](references/pipeline-and-failures.md) for the review procedure.
- Request a body/underlayer and independent open garment when cloth is required. A fused dressed mesh is a prototype input, not evidence that it contains a hidden body or usable garment. Segmentation may separate labels while leaving missing surfaces and fused seams.

## Validate in stages

1. **Unrigged asset:** inspect face and proportions from front, side, back and three-quarter; hands and feet close up. Inventory geometry, topology, textures, roughness, skeleton and animation data. Reject major identity or anatomical defects before expensive fitting.
2. **Skeleton and weights:** fit the donor rest joints to the actual mesh, preserve required names/hierarchy, regenerate inverse binds, then transfer weights. Loose garment slices can place anatomical joints inside the torso: inspect shoulder/elbow centres and left/right symmetry directly. Avoid hard colour-based weight overrides across a continuous garment; ink and texture boundaries are not anatomical seams. Use the identical fitted rest skeleton in export and preview. Verify numerical binding and visible deformation separately.
3. **Finger articulation:** verify each digit chain lies inside its finger and every phalanx has meaningful vertex influence. Exercise open, relaxed and curled poses and inspect mesh displacement. A moving bone, nonzero total hand weights, or passing normalized-weight checks does not prove articulated fingers.
   For generated faces, measure mouth, eyelid and jaw landmarks on the actual normalized mesh before adding morphs or shader features; never borrow donor coordinates or estimate from full-body views. Compare isolated closed/open mouth and blink poses with the head held still. Speech must not move the nose, upper face, throat, scarf or collar: check both upper and lower boundaries of morph influence. Use guides and displacement-region checks, then inspect natural speech from front and side.
4. **Cloth and hidden surfaces:** inspect the independently moving garment from both sides and beneath the hem. Fit collision shapes to the rendered clothed body, not just anatomical joint positions. Test walking, jogging, turning and sitting. Numerical clearance is necessary but cannot establish natural drape or complete mesh separation.
5. **Game rendering:** reuse the repo's real material/G-buffer, ink composite, shadows and final antialiasing. Preserve textures and repair materials. Provide a standard/stylized comparison without resetting camera, animation or cloth. Identify fixed preview lighting versus world-specific appearance.
6. **Asset budget before committing:** measure shipped bytes, triangles/vertices, draw calls/materials, texture dimensions and estimated decoded GPU memory including mipmaps. Set a budget for the character’s actual screen size, role, simultaneous instances and target devices. Do not ship the generator’s full export by default: preserve it as source, strip unused payload, reduce oversized textures, remove permanently hidden geometry from role-specific derivatives, and consider LOD/decimation where needed. Preserve UV seams, skin weights, fingers and face detail; regenerate vertex-indexed data after topology changes. Compare the optimized result against the full asset in the same camera/pose and real game scene, then rerun deformation checks. Record before/after measurements and remaining device-profile gaps; triangle count or desktop FPS alone does not prove mobile performance. See the father example in [pipeline-and-failures.md](references/pipeline-and-failures.md).
7. **Acceptance:** play complete real animation cycles and transitions in the shared game/review path, including idle → talk → idle and the role’s other required motions. Inspect front, side and rear at several phases, with neutral clay/unlit as well as final shading; do not accept a rig from a flattering still, bind-pose check or individually moving bones. Check shoulders, armpits, lapels, wrists and UV seams for stretching/tears, and quantify edge stretch or seam separation where it catches a demonstrated failure. Run appropriate rig, topology, motion and build checks. State what is actually exported, what exists only at runtime, and what remains imperfect.
   Isolate layered drivers too: hold the same base animation/time and compare left/right gaze, then closed/open mouth. A head glance must not drag the torso or shoulders unless that body turn is intentional. Avoid stacking procedural torso turns or speech nods onto an already animated performance without inspecting the combined result. Regression tests should check unaffected regions and bones as well as the parts meant to move.
   Trace control-to-bone retargeting before changing gaze: a control named "head" may drive the neck and carry clothing with it. Check skinned collar, scarf and chest vertex displacement at matched clip times, not just torso bone transforms. Keep the jaw on the skull side of the weight transition. Paused diagnostic controls must apply without advancing the base animation, so comparisons cannot hide shifts behind natural body motion.
   Check the jaw underside in profile: the chin and rear skull need different attachment boundaries, and a horizontal cutoff can pass an upper-jaw test while shearing the chin. Stress combined yaw/pitch through the supported range and measure compression as well as stretch. If a local runtime correction is needed, preserve UV seams and pinned clothing, test rest reset/repeated poses/world transforms, and measure animation CPU cost separately from render submission. Continue repairing defects exposed by those reviews; a passing targeted test is not the end of a visual repair pass.

Make reversible fixes autonomously within the user's authorized scope. When a generation or heuristic repeatedly fails, identify the structural cause and change the input or asset contract instead of stacking cosmetic patches. Never claim a production-ready character on the strength of a screenshot, collider test, joint names or weight normalization alone.
